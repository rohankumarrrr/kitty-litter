# The Kitty Litter

A window for everything the kittens are doing, laid out like Slack: Activity and Threads at the top, then #general and a channel for each kitten, then a DM with the cat, each on its Rimlight tile in the sidebar, which sits flush on the window's chrome. The place you're looking at, and a thread beside it, share one page panel inset from the window, with its toolbar at the top. Each T3 session is a thread: a channel shows its parent with a reply bar, and clicking it opens the thread in a pane beside the channel. Messages are bubbles, yours on the right and each kitten's on the left in its colour. Typing in it sends only your words, and nothing in it looks at your screen. Any message's words can be selected and copied, and it's all in Opal Horizon, its one look.

## Sub-features

- `messages-activity` lists approvals, questions and inbox reports, then what each kitten has in progress, and counts what's waiting in the sidebar.
- `messages-threads` lists every thread you've written in, unread ones first, each with its channel, parent, latest reply and how many more.
- `messages-general` sends your words through the router. The cat's own answer is a plain message after yours, work it starts is a thread under your message, and words it sends on to a session are a reply there, also sent to #general.
- `messages-channel` shows a kitten's threads as parents with reply bars, bold when it's finished something you haven't seen and with a red count of what it's waiting on you for. Its box starts a new thread, which opens in the pane.
- `messages-thread` shows one thread in a pane beside the channel: its parent, how many replies, each reply, what it's waiting on, and a box that replies only into it, with "Also send to #channel" under it. Dragging the line between the place and the pane resizes the pane, with the left-right resize pointer over it, as Slack's does. The width stays for every thread, as the window resizes and across a launch, never leaving either side too narrow to read: the thread's pane at least 320 points wide and the place at least 360. Until you drag it, the pane is 45% of the panel, held to 320...460.
- `messages-shades` shows what you've asked the cat itself, each talk a thread, and its box goes through the router as #general's does, with the cat answering there. Work you started with the cat outside The Kitty Litter, such as a fix, is a thread there too, and takes a reply. While The Kitty Litter is on your phone, its thread has **Show on phone** at the top, which [phone.md](phone.md) covers as `phone-switch`.
- `messages-shortcuts` takes T3 Code's, the Claude app's and Slack's keys while The Kitty Litter has the keyboard: the thread, place or unread channel above or below, back and forward, a sidebar row by number, Threads, Activity, a jump box, Esc, the place's box, closing, and a list of them all from ⌘/ or the link at the foot of the sidebar.
- `messages-unread` draws a red New line over where what you haven't seen starts, in a channel and in a thread's pane, with "↑ N new messages" at the top while it's above what you can see, and Mark unread on a message, on your Mac and on a thread's page on your phone.
- `messages-mention` sends words in #general that @ a kitten straight to it, in a new thread under them there, with no router turn, and the line over the box names the kitten as you type.
- `messages-actions` floats a bar of icons over a message's top right corner while you hover it or Tab to it: React, Reply on a parent, Copy, Mark unread and Open in T3, each with its name as a tooltip, and More for what doesn't fit in a narrow pane. It covers what's under it, and the name, time and words never move for it.
- `messages-reactions` marks a message with 👍, ✅, 👀, ❤️, 😂 or 🎉 from React in its bar, shown under it with a smiling face after them to add another, where pressing one takes it off. On the phone, tapping a message's words offers them. Nothing is sent.
- `messages-search` finds a message by its words with ⌘F, newest first, in what The Kitty Litter has read and, once typing pauses, older threads T3 names that The Kitty Litter then reads. It matches the words a message shows, not its markdown. Opening one opens its thread or channel, scrolls to it and marks it for a moment. On the phone, **Search** does the same over what a phone can open.
- `messages-delivery` shows your words faded with "Sending…" where the time goes until T3 has them, and if they never get there, why under them, with Try again.
- `messages-formatting` draws a message's markdown as it was written: lists, tables, quotes and code blocks with Copy, and a link to a pull request as a chip with its state. Pressing a link shows its whole address in a bar over the box, and only Open opens it.
- `messages-select` selects words in a message, which gives the window the keyboard without making The Kitty Litter the active app, and ⌘C copies them as plain words. Selected words read on any bubble, with or without the keyboard.
- `messages-picture` puts your picture beside your messages, round, where a kitten's cat goes, and in place of your square in reply bars and Threads. With none, yours look as they always have.
- `messages-opal` draws the window, and the cat's own windows, in Opal Horizon, The Kitty Litter's one look, as `docs/design.md` says.

## How to get to it (user POV)

- Press ⌃⌘B, or choose The Kitty Litter from the cat's menu.
- Click a row in the sidebar, such as Threads or a kitten's channel, to change what's showing.
- Click a thread's parent, its reply bar, or a "replied to a thread" line to open the thread beside the channel. ✕, or Esc in its box, closes it.
- Type in a box and press Return. Tick "Also send to #channel" first to show a reply in the channel too.
- Press in a message's words and drag, then ⌘C or right-click and Copy.
- Press a link in a message, then Open or Cancel in the bar over the box. Press Copy on a code block.
- Click into The Kitty Litter, then press a shortcut such as ⌘⇧] or ⌘K. **⌘/ Shortcuts**, at the bottom of the sidebar, lists them all.
- Press ⌘F in The Kitty Litter and type a word or two, then Return or click a message. On the phone, tap **Search** at the top of any page.

- **Load older.** Journey `journeys/older.txt`. `busy` gives each kitten 40 threads and the DM 32 talks, so The Kitty Litter reads 30 of each. `channel steve` then `scroll -100000` goes to the top of Steve's channel: `2-loading` shows "Loading older messages…" over the oldest of his newest 30, and `3-loaded`, a few seconds later, shows his 10 older threads in above what was on the screen, with the lowest posts in the same place, such as "Make it shorter, please.", and no more to load. `channel shades` and the same scroll load the DM's last 2 talks, and `6-dm-top` starts at the oldest talk. What's kept across a restart, such as a reply also sent to a channel, can't be driven here: `testAReplyAlsoSentToAChannelIsStillThereAfterARestart` in `FlowTests` starts a second controller from the first one's saved inbox.
- **Speed.** Journey `journeys/perf.txt`. `busy` adds hundreds of made-up threads, with threads of hundreds of messages, and `perf` times what you'd feel from inside the app: closing The Kitty Litter and opening it again (`reopen`, whose `reading=false` and `rows=` say it showed the channel rather than a note that it's reading), switching to each place, opening a long thread in the pane beside its channel (`open-thread`), going from it to another (`switch-thread`) and closing it (`close-thread`), the shortcuts (`key-thread` stepping up a channel's threads, `key-place` down and up the sidebar, `key-back` back and forward, and `key-jump` opening the jump box and what it found, with `jump-filter` typing a name), scrolling that long thread 40 points at a time, a message arriving, a refresh with nothing new, typing and resizing. Each line is `perf <step> median=… p95=… max=…`, and switching lines say how many rows the place has and how many are views in the window (`rows=` and `inWindow=`). Run it with `swift run -c release`, since the app you install is a release build. The demo's T3 answers at once, so for how long real reads take, run `swift run shades --channels-check`, which reads your real T3 once, closes and opens again a second later, and prints only counts and milliseconds: `first words`, `reopened and shown` and `reopened and caught up`. `scroll <points>` moves the place that's showing the way scrolling would, up when it's negative, so pictures can show older rows coming in as you go up.
- **Canned pictures.** `swift run shades --demo --channels-image <folder>` draws every place and a thread in the pane from canned data, in Opal, including a thread from #general that's waiting on you, Threads with an unread thread, and the narrowest window with a thread open, alone and with a reply's bar showing (`narrow-thread-hover`), drawn without the real pointer. `thread-dragged` is a thread's pane dragged to 440 points, and `narrow-thread-dragged` the same width chosen in the narrowest window, where the pane gives way to 320 so the channel keeps its 360. A drag itself, and the width kept across closing, reopening, resizing and a launch, are `testADraggedThreadPaneKeepsItsWidthWithinLimitsAndAcrossALaunch` in `ChannelsWindowTests`, which hands the edge its drags straight, so nothing reaches the desktop. The bar is also drawn over a kitten's parent with its reply bar (`channel-hover-replies`), over yours (`channel-hover-yours`), and over a reply with reactions, wide and narrow (`thread-hover-reactions`, `narrow-thread-hover-reactions`), where it folds into More beside the name and time.

## Gotchas

- Opal is dark only, so the demo has no light or dark to pick. `opal <folder>` draws a sheet of its primitives, from `journeys/opal.txt`, and `opal <folder> <hex>...` adds a row of cats in those jackets.
- `key` gives The Kitty Litter the keyboard first, as a click into it does, so `windows` says `channels ... key=true` afterwards, and `front` stays your app. `key close` closes The Kitty Litter, the same as ⌘W.
- `highlight` gives The Kitty Litter the keyboard, as pressing in words does, so `windows` says `channels ... key=true` and `active=true` afterwards. The app in front stays yours.
- `channel` takes `activity`, `threads`, `general`, `shades` or the start of a kitten's name, such as `steve`, and closes any thread that's open. `thread` opens the newest thread the place shows, or with words, the newest whose parent has them, and prints its ID. `back` closes the pane, the same as its ✕. `also on|off` ticks "Also send to #channel" under an open thread's box.
- `draft <words>` types into the box that's showing without sending, the way typing does, so the line over it can be drawn. `react <name>` picks that reaction from React on the last message that takes one, one of `thumbsUp`, `check`, `eyes`, `heart`, `laugh` and `tada`, and prints which message, or prints `nothing takes a reaction`. A message T3 hasn't echoed yet takes none, so react a few seconds after sending.
- `t3 off` is T3 not running: replies fail, and The Kitty Litter says it can't reach T3 until `t3 on`. `t3 stuck` is T3 never answering, as when signing in waits on a Keychain dialog. `retry` presses Try again under the last message that has one, or prints `nothing to try again`.
- `messages` is the same as ⌃⌘B, so a second one closes the window, unless something has covered it since. `channels` only opens it.
- The window is live, so a row under the real pointer can draw highlighted. That's where the mouse is, not a bug.
- `send` is the same as typing in a box and pressing Return: the open thread's, or the place's when no thread is open. It never takes the keyboard. The box beeps and sends nothing when there's nowhere to send, such as in a new thread before T3 has started it. Activity and Threads have no box.
- The Kitty Litter reads the canned transcripts when it opens. Draw before `wait 1` and it can still say it's reading.
- The window opens where it was last left. Its frame in `windows` can be on another display.
